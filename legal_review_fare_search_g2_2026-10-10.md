## 3. Regime A (new): U.S. DOT "ticket agent" rules

**What the law says.** Under 49 U.S.C. § 40102(a)(45), a ticket agent is a person, other than an air carrier or its employee, that as a principal or agent sells, offers for sale, negotiates for, or "holds itself out as selling, providing, or arranging for air transportation." (Statutory text as quoted in DOT Order 2013-8-8; I did not open the statute itself.)

**The enforcement precedent that matters most.** DOT Order 2013-8-8 (Hipmunk, Inc., served August 20, 2013):
- Hipmunk told DOT it was not a ticket agent because it sold no tickets and visitors bought directly from airlines. DOT's enforcement office disagreed and said that Hipmunk "arranges for the sale of air transportation and it receives commissions for doing so." Hipmunk agreed not to contest that conclusion for the purposes of the settlement, while reserving the right to challenge it later.
- The violations were display violations on the site's itinerary and fare screens: missing code-share (operating carrier) disclosure under 14 CFR 257.5(d) and 49 U.S.C. § 41712(c), and a missing baggage-fee notice under 14 CFR 399.85(b) on the first screen offering a fare quotation for a specific itinerary.
- Penalty: $30,000 in compromise, with $15,000 payable only if Hipmunk violated the order within a year.

**Related rules.** DOT materials say the full-fare advertising rule (14 CFR 399.84) applies to airlines and ticket agents that advertise fares, including on websites; DOT consent orders against ticket agents have enforced it on internet fare displays.

**Is metasearch covered today?** Unclear.
- In 2014 DOT proposed to codify that websites with flight metasearch engines, and similar intermediaries, are ticket agents if compensated in connection with the sale of air transportation. Google, Kayak, Skyscanner and others argued against it in comments (PhocusWire coverage), saying they surface information and link to the seller.
- DOT's 2016 "Enhancing Airline Passenger Protections III" final rule did not address that definition; DOT said it would address it in a later rule.
- **I could not confirm whether DOT ever finalized that definition, or whether the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024 changed the statute.** A House bill text I found would include intermediaries that hold themselves out as a source of information about or reservations for air transportation; I could not confirm what was enacted. Travel Tech (an industry trade group, so an advocacy source) said in February 2024 that metasearch engines "are at times categorized as Ticket Agents."

**How this applies to Sparkfare.**
- **Today (existing exposure, outside G2):** Sparkfare shows prices and earns commissions through links. Under the Hipmunk reasoning, a regulator could argue the site arranges the sale. Sparkfare's "we don't sell or book travel" copy helps with state-law positioning but did not save Hipmunk, whose own position was the same. I could not confirm whether the cached prices Sparkfare displays (from the Travelpayouts Data API) include taxes and fees, which the full-fare rule requires for an advertised fare.
- **With Fare Search (increase):** (1) the live results are fare quotations for specific itineraries, the screens DOT's notices attach to; (2) those screens are generated by Travelpayouts under Sparkfare's brand, so Sparkfare may carry exposure for notices it cannot control; (3) a branded "Sparkfare" search increases the "holds itself out" signal.
- **Whether the White Label results screen already shows the required notices is unknown.** The new White Label version shows baggage badges and fare details per the Travelpayouts versions article, but nothing I read says it meets DOT's code-share and baggage-notice rules.

**Severity (my qualitative assessment):** medium, uncertain. Precedent penalties are small, but the regime has specific display obligations and the operator is a sole proprietor with no liability shield.

## 4. Regime B: state seller-of-travel (California is the main one)

**Text verified this session (FindLaw, updated Jan 1, 2026):** California Bus. & Prof. Code § 17550.1(a) defines a seller of travel as a person who sells, provides, furnishes, contracts for, arranges, or "advertises that he or she can or may arrange, or has arranged" air or sea transportation. Legislative analyses confirm a seller is deemed to do business in California if it solicits prospective purchasers located there. The project's 2026-10-07 analysis (Washington, Hawaii, Florida text and the Iowa and Nevada repeals) was not re-verified this session.

**The premise of your 2026-10-07 decision.** The decision rests on the position that Sparkfare publishes fare information and sends people to the booking site, and does not sell, book, ticket, arrange or take payment for travel. Fare Search keeps the last four parts true. It strains the first, because users now see multi-agency fare results inside a Sparkfare-branded search before they leave.

**Change in exposure (my assessment):** modest increase in California, with no case law or regulator guidance to calibrate it. The federal finding above matters here because it is evidence that a regulator can treat a commission-earning fare-display site as "arranging" even when users buy elsewhere. Your reopen triggers T1 to T5 are not tripped by Fare Search on their face (no money held, no itinerary-building service, no hotel bundling). A new trigger is proposed below.

**Hawaii, Florida, Washington:** no change on their face; Hawaii's "intermediary" language and Florida's "directly or indirectly" language are marginally closer with a branded multi-agency search. Not re-verified.

## 6. Affiliate disclosure, price claims and privacy

- **Disclosure placement (FTC Endorsement Guides, per the 2026-09-26 review; not re-verified this session).** The sole mechanism the project judged inadequate in September was a nav link to `disclosure.html`; the fix (roadmap step 16 and the referral-copy pass) puts disclosure next to the link. Fare Search adds a page you may not be able to put text on. If the results page cannot show a disclosure, the Sparkfare button and interstitial carry it, and users who arrive at the results via a shared URL see none. Whether that is adequate is a judgment call; I flag it as a moderate gap.
- **Price claims.** Sparkfare's "From $X, as of" is a cached "lowest fare seen" value; Fare Search shows live prices that will often differ. The existing "as of" wording plus the interstitial line in Task C addresses this if it ships; it is also a consumer-deception risk if not.
- **Privacy.** The White Label is operated by Travelpayouts on your subdomain and may set cookies and receive search inputs. Update `privacy.html` before any traffic (already in the plan as E4). The trip UUID passed as sub-ID is not personal data on its face, but it links to an account inside Sparkfare's own database. I did not review Travelpayouts' cookie behavior.
- **Entity.** No liability-shielding entity exists (roadmap step 18, last known open as of 2026-09-26). Every regime above reaches a sole proprietor personally.
