# After You Return: Restarting Mail, Checking the Home, and Handling Issues

*Last reviewed: October 16, 2026*

Returning home after an extended trip should be a relief, not a surprise. When a property has sat unoccupied for several weeks or months, walking in the front door requires a few methodical checks before you drop your luggage and start unpacking.

Here is a practical, step-by-step checklist for re-entering your home, safely re-pressurizing plumbing, resuming mail delivery, and knowing what to do if you discover an issue.

---

### Step 1: The Initial 10-Minute Walkthrough

Before bringing all your luggage inside, do a quick, conscious walk through the perimeter and interior of the home:

#### 1. The Exterior Perimeter Walk
* Check all ground-level windows and exterior doors to confirm latches, screens, and locks are intact and show no signs of tampering.
* Look at outdoor spigots, garden beds, and downspouts for unusual standing water or erosion.
* Glance up at rooflines and gutters for fallen branches or dislodged trim.

#### 2. The Interior "Smell and Sound" Check
As soon as you step inside, pause and pay attention:
* **Gas Odor:** If you smell the rotten-egg odor of natural gas (mercaptan), **do not turn on or off any light switches, do not use a mobile phone inside, and leave the house immediately**. Call your local gas utility or 911 from a safe distance outside.
* **Musty or Damp Smells:** A strong earthy or musty odor often signals a slow leak, hidden moisture behind walls, or a dry P-trap (see below).
* **Sounds of Running Water:** Listen carefully for humming pipes, hissing supply lines, or running toilet valves.

---

### Step 2: Restoring Water and Re-Pressurizing Pipes

If you turned off the main water valve before departing, re-opening it correctly is critical to prevent plumbing damage:

1. **Re-Open the Main Valve Slowly:**
   * Never snap a main quarter-turn ball valve open rapidly, and do not spin a gate valve open at full speed.
   * Opening a dry or drained system too quickly sends a violent shockwave (water hammer) through your pipes, which can loosen solder joints, blow out flexible appliance hoses, or dislodge sediment into faucet aerators.
   * Open the valve a quarter of the way, wait 30 seconds until you hear water fill the main lines and the sound of rushing water subsides, then open the valve completely.
2. **Purge Air from the Faucets:**
   * Air trapped in the plumbing will sputter and spit violently when faucets are first turned on.
   * Slowly open the highest cold water faucet in the house (such as an upstairs bathroom sink) until a smooth, steady stream flows.
   * Repeat across all sinks, tubs, and showers, letting water run for 1 to 2 minutes to flush stale water that has sat in stagnant pipes.
3. **Refill Dried-Out P-Traps:**
   * If a home sits unoccupied for weeks in dry or heated weather, the water barrier in sink P-traps, shower drains, and floor drains can evaporate completely, allowing sewer gas into the living area.
   * Run water for 30 seconds in every sink, bathtub, shower, and laundry basin, and flush every toilet once to restore the water seals in the traps.
4. **Inspect for Drips:**
   * Once the system is under full pressure, inspect under every kitchen and bathroom sink with a flashlight. Look around toilet fill valves, the water heater base, and washing machine connections to confirm no fittings have developed small drips.

---

### Step 3: Reactivating Other Home Systems

* **Thermostat:** Switch your thermostat from baseline vacation settings back to your regular comfort schedule.
* **Water Heater:** If your water heater was placed on "Vacation" mode or dialed down, return the thermostat dial to normal operating temperature (typically 120°F / 49°C for standard residential units).
* **Refrigerator Ice Maker:** Turn the ice maker feeler arm back down or toggle the switch on. Discard the first one to two batches of ice cubes to clear stale lines.

---

### Step 4: Restarting Mail and Deliveries

* **USPS Hold Mail:** If you placed a standard USPS Hold Mail request:
  * If you requested delivery resumption, your carrier will deliver all accumulated mail on the end date specified in your request.
  * If your mail does not arrive on the scheduled date, check the digital confirmation number in the original email sent by USPS. Occasionally, large bins of accumulated mail cannot fit in a standard delivery vehicle and must be picked up directly at your local Post Office branch.
* **Temporary Forwarding:** If you submitted a temporary Change of Address or used a forwarding service for an absence over 30 days, confirm your forwarding end date. If you returned earlier than planned, log into your USPS.com account to cancel or update the forwarding timeline.

---

### Step 5: What to Do if Something Went Wrong

#### If You Find an Active Water Leak
1. **Shut off the main water valve immediately.** Do not investigate the source before cutting the supply.
2. **Document everything before cleanup:** Take wide-angle and close-up photographs and video of standing water, damaged ceilings, flooring, and affected personal property.
3. **Mitigate further damage:** Once documented, take reasonable steps to prevent mold (pump out standing water, open windows, run fans, and elevate furniture).
4. **Contact your insurance carrier promptly:** Report the date you discovered the loss and provide your photographic documentation.

#### If You Suspect Unauthorized Entry or Vandalism
1. **Do not enter the residence:** An intruder could still be on the premises.
2. **Call local police:** File an official police incident report; your insurance carrier will require the police report number for any vandalism or burglary claim.
3. **Document before touching anything:** Take photos of broken windows, forced doors, or disturbed property.
