Sale fares

Is this sale fare actually good?

A sale label tells you a fare is lower than something. This guide shows a plainer test: compare the price with the lowest fares Sparkfare has cached for that same route over the last 30 days. It needs no signup and works for any round-trip fare on a route Sparkfare tracks. Sparkfare doesn't sell flights and doesn't predict where fares are heading.

Six steps

  1. Note the round-trip price. Write down the round-trip price in US dollars exactly as shown. The checker doesn't handle one-way or multi-city fares, so compare a round trip with a round trip.
  2. Enter the route. In the price checker, choose your home airport and destination, then type the price. If a route isn't covered, the checker says it doesn't track it yet.
  3. Read the verdict. The checker answers Deal, Typical or High. It also shows the median of the lowest daily fares it cached over the last 30 days, how many daily fares that median comes from, and the dates they cover.
  4. Check how much history there is. If the checker says it hasn't seen enough fares for a route, read that as "can't tell yet", not as good or bad.
  5. Keep the limits in mind. The history is the lowest fare cached each day for the route. It isn't tied to your dates, airline or fare class, so a sale fare for specific dates can sit above or below what the checker compares it with. A verdict is a comparison, not a quote.
  6. Decide for yourself. A verdict says where a price sits against its own recent history. It doesn't say what the fare will do next, and whether it fits your trip is your call.
