## Promotional property description

> Sparkfare is a flight-deal alert site. We track flight prices daily against each route's own
> 30-day price history and email subscribers when a fare is genuinely, measurably cheaper than
> normal — not just "cheap-looking," but statistically below what that specific route has recently
> cost. Once someone acts on a deal alert and has a trip booked, we shift into "Away Mode": a
> curated set of pre-trip services (travel insurance, luggage storage, mail holding, connectivity,
> and similar) presented as "everything else, handled," so the traveler doesn't have to hunt for
> those separately before leaving. Language learning fits naturally into that same pre-trip moment
> for travelers heading somewhere their destination's primary language isn't their own.

## How we plan to promote Rocket Languages

> We'd promote Rocket Languages within the Away Mode flow, surfaced only for **international
> destinations** — we know the destination for every tracked trip, so this wouldn't be shown
> indiscriminately on domestic routes where it isn't relevant. Three real, existing touchpoints:
>
> - A dedicated `/away-mode` page on the site, listing our current partner services (currently
>   SafetyWing, Bounce, US Global Mail, AirHelp, and Yesim).
> - A follow-up email sent immediately after a user books a tracked flight.
> - Two later lifecycle emails: a reminder a few days after booking, and a departure briefing
>   roughly a week before the trip.
>
> We also publish destination-specific content where a language-learning link is a natural,
> contextually relevant addition rather than a sitewide banner: a growing blog of city/destination
> guides, and a set of programmatic origin/destination pages (`/data/*`) covering our tracked
> routes. Placing Rocket Languages against a specific destination guide (e.g. a guide for a
> non-English-speaking country) lets the pitch be contextual instead of generic.
