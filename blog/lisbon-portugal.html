Lisbon, Portugal: when to go, and what it actually costs

Destination guide

Lisbon's sweet spot arrives in May and September — crowds thin, prices drop, and the golden light flatters every azulejo-tiled corner. Sparkfare files Lisbon under Cluster 2 ("Shoulder-Season Cliffs"), meaning it's one of the routes where a genuine deal needs to clear at least 15% below its own 30-day average before we badge it — no marketing-team judgment call, just that math, explained in full in how we rank deals.

What it actually costs on the ground

  • A proper dinner with wine runs €18–25 per person in Alfama's neighborhood spots — heritage-listed tascas serving Michelin-caliber food at market rates.
  • A pastel de nata costs €1.50 at the bakeries stacked three-deep on Rua da Rosa; a cortado follows for €0.95.
  • A transit day-pass is €6.40 — tram 28 and the ferry crossing to Belém both included, full city access for less than a single hotel breakfast upcharge.

Before you lock in dates

Secure your baseline first: travel insurance that covers medical and trip interruption, a house sitter or pet-care plan if you need one, and an eSIM or data plan — Portugal's WiFi is solid in restaurants but genuinely spotty up in Sintra's hills. A deal on the flight only compounds if the logistics around it don't unravel; that's exactly what our Away Mode checklist is for.
