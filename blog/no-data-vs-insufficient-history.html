"No data" vs. "insufficient history": two different honest states

Methodology

Scroll far enough down the board and you'll occasionally see a route that isn't priced at all, sitting apart from the dimmed "Building history" cards covered in an earlier post. These look similar at a glance, but they mean genuinely different things, and we keep them separate rather than folding one into the other.

no_data: nothing came back today

This route's fetch simply didn't return a usable price — no route match, no fare found, or a temporary gap in the underlying flight-search data. We don't know the current price, and we don't guess one. If this persists, it's usually a genuinely thin or unusual route, not a bug — but we'd rather show "no data" honestly than quietly drop the route from the board without explanation.

insufficient_history: we have a price, just not enough of them yet

This is the "Building history" state from our earlier post — a route with a real, current price, but fewer than the 10 distinct days of accumulated history, spanning at least 14 days, required before we'll call it a deal or rule it out. The price shown is genuine; the judgment about whether it's a good one just isn't ready yet.

Why the distinction matters

Collapsing these into one generic "no data" bucket would hide a real difference: one route has no current price at all, the other has a real price waiting on more history. Treating them the same would either overstate confidence in a route we haven't actually watched long enough, or understate what we do already know about a route that's simply new to fresh data. Full detail on the history requirement itself is in what "Building history" means.
