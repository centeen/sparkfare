Leaving a Cat for Two to Three Weeks: Options Compared

Away Mode · Last reviewed 2026-10-16

A weekend away is one thing; two to three weeks away is another. While cats are often viewed as self-sufficient, an absence of fourteen to twenty-one days requires deliberate care planning.

Because cats are territorial and sensitive to changes in their territory, removing them from their normal surroundings often causes significant stress. Here is how the three main care options compare, along with practical preparations needed before departure.

The three options compared

1. Daily Drop-In Sitter (Visiting Once or Twice a Day)

A trusted friend, neighbor, or professional sitter visits your home on a set schedule.

  • Best for: Most healthy adult cats who prefer staying in their familiar territory.
  • Frequency: Animal welfare organizations and feline veterinary guidelines advise that someone visit at least once every 24 hours, and ideally twice daily. Automatic feeders and gravity water bowls can malfunction, jam, or lose power, and litter boxes require daily cleaning over a multi-week span to prevent soiling outside the box.
  • Key advantages: Your cat remains in their familiar environment with standard routines, scents, and sleeping spots.
  • Key drawbacks: Extended periods alone between visits. For highly social cats, three weeks of brief daily visits can lead to boredom or stress behaviors.

2. Live-In Housesitter

A friend, family member, or sitter stays at your home while you are away.

  • Best for: Senior cats, kittens, cats requiring scheduled medications (such as daily insulin or thyroid medication), or cats that experience distress when left alone for long stretches.
  • Key advantages: Continuous human presence, standard feeding routines, and immediate observation if a medical problem arises. Your home is also occupied.
  • Key drawbacks: Requires coordinating home access, guest arrangements, and setting clear household boundaries.

3. Boarding Facility or Cattery

Your cat stays in an external pet care facility or veterinary boarding kennel.

  • Best for: Situations where home access is not feasible, or cats with complex medical needs requiring continuous veterinary monitoring.
  • Key advantages: Professional staff on site throughout the day, controlled environment, and dedicated housing.
  • Key drawbacks: Higher environmental stress for many cats due to unfamiliar scents, sounds, and proximity to strange animals. Vaccines must be updated well in advance (typically including FVRCP and rabies, and occasionally feline leukemia depending on facility policy).

Practical preparations for a 2- to 3-week trip

1. Establish Veterinary Authorization in Writing

Do not wait for an emergency while you are travelling across time zones:

  • Contact your primary veterinary clinic before departure.
  • Leave a written letter authorising your sitter or caregiver to seek medical evaluation if needed.
  • Specify a pre-authorised spending limit on your card if your clinic allows keeping one on file.
  • Provide the name, address, and telephone number of the nearest 24-hour emergency animal hospital.

2. Stock More Food and Litter Than You Expect to Use

  • Purchase enough of your cat's standard food, treats, and preferred litter for four full weeks. Do not introduce new diets or litter textures right before leaving, as dietary changes can trigger gastrointestinal upset.
  • Leave an extra, clean litter box in the home if you will be using drop-in visits, providing extra capacity between visits.

3. Carrier and Microchip Accessibility

  • Keep your cat carrier assembled, clean, and in plain sight rather than stored in an attic or closet. If your caregiver needs to transport your cat in an emergency, they should not have to search for the carrier.
  • Confirm that your cat's microchip contact details (address, mobile number, and secondary emergency contact) are up to date in the national pet registry.
