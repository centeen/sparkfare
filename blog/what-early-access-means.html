What "early access" actually means when you refer a friend

Away Mode

If you've signed up for alerts, you'll see a "Share Sparkfare & get early access" link below the signup bar. It's easy to assume that's marketing shorthand for "you'll be listed first" or some cosmetic reorder. It isn't — it's a genuinely earlier send time.

How it actually works

Your share link is a plain URL with your own id attached. When someone signs up for the first time through that link — never on a resubmit, only a genuinely new signup — both of you get flagged for early access. It's a one-time flag, not a counter: referring more friends after the first doesn't do anything further for you.

"Ahead of the general send" is literally true

Early-access users get the daily digest from a genuinely separate, earlier scheduled job — a full hour before the regular run reaches everyone else. It's not the same email reordered or resent; it's an actually earlier delivery, and if you're already marked as sent for the day from the early run, the later general run simply skips you rather than sending twice.

Why we built it this way

A referral reward that isn't real doesn't earn trust — same reasoning behind writing about a "Building history" state instead of hiding it, or naming a partner as pending instead of implying it's live. If we're going to say "early access," it needs to be a real hour, not a phrase.
